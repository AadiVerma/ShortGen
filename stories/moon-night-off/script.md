# The night the moon took the night off

<!-- Format: original bedtime story for young children (about 4 to 8). Written for this
     project after the first children's story was judged, fairly, to be lame: it had a
     lesson and no idea.

     This one starts from an idea instead: the moon is tired, takes a night off, and
     everyone who tries to replace her fails in a funny way, until the smallest one
     does a smaller job well. The last line turns the story toward the child's own
     room. Nothing is explained and no moral is stated.

     The hero is a firefly on purpose. On screen he is a dot of light, so he cannot
     come out looking different from one picture to the next, which is what spoiled
     the owl. The little girl is never shown either: the model draws children badly.

     Picture notes from the tests. Asking for a moon "in a night sky full of stars"
     drew a dozen moons, and a style that mentioned the sky put a moon behind the bear
     on the one night there is not supposed to be one. "Huge, filling the picture"
     then drew a shapeless blob. So the three moon scenes all ask for "one cute round
     pale yellow full moon character", the style no longer mentions the sky, and the
     scenes in between are set under trees or indoors, where no sky shows.

     "Shh" is written "Hush" because the voice model spells "Shh" out letter by letter. -->

One night, the moon said: I am so tired. I have not had a night off in four billion years.
<!-- image: one cute round pale yellow full moon character with a sleepy face, half-closed eyes and a big yawn, centered, dark sky -->
<!-- camera: zoom_in -->

So she pulled a cloud up to her chin, and went to sleep.
<!-- image: one cute round pale yellow full moon character fast asleep, eyes closed, a fluffy white cloud over it like a blanket, centered, dark sky -->

Down below, it got dark. Really dark.
<!-- image: a pitch black forest at night, only many pairs of round cartoon eyes blinking in the darkness -->

Who will be the moon? The frog held up a plate. The bear held up a pancake. Then he ate it.
<!-- image: a big friendly brown cartoon bear holding one round golden pancake above its head with both paws, dark tree trunks behind -->
<!-- camera: pan_up -->

A tiny firefly said, I can do it. Everybody laughed. You are smaller than a crumb!
<!-- image: one tiny glowing yellow firefly hovering alone between big dark tree trunks at night -->

But in one window, a little girl could not sleep.
<!-- image: close view of a small wooden window of a cozy house at night, curtains closed, one tiny glowing firefly beside it -->
<!-- camera: zoom_in -->

So the firefly flew up to her ceiling, and stayed there. All night.
<!-- image: a dark cozy bedroom at night, a small bed with a blanket, one tiny warm dot of light glowing on the ceiling above it -->

He was not the moon. But he was enough.
<!-- image: extreme close-up of one tiny firefly glowing warmly on a dark ceiling, soft circle of golden light around it -->
<!-- camera: zoom_in -->

The next night, the moon came back. But the firefly kept his job.
<!-- image: one cute round pale yellow full moon character with a big happy smile, centered, dark sky above small rooftops -->

Look up. See that tiny light in your room? Hush. He is working.
<!-- image: a dark cozy bedroom ceiling at night with one tiny warm dot of light, soft moonlight through the window -->
<!-- camera: zoom_in -->
