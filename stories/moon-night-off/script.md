# The night the moon took the night off

<!-- Format: original bedtime story for young children (about 4 to 8). Written for this
     project after the first children's story was judged, fairly, to be lame: it had a
     lesson and no idea.

     This one starts from an idea instead: the moon is tired, takes a night off, and
     everyone who tries to replace her fails in a funny way, until the smallest one
     does a smaller job well. The last line turns the story toward the child's own
     room. Nothing is explained and no moral is stated.

     The hero is a firefly on purpose. On screen he is a dot of light, so he cannot
     come out looking different from one picture to the next, which is what spoiled
     the owl. The little girl is never shown either: the model draws children badly.

     Picture notes from the tests. Asking for a moon "in a night sky full of stars"
     drew a dozen moons, and a style that mentioned the sky put a moon behind the bear
     on the one night there is not supposed to be one. "Huge, filling the picture"
     then drew a shapeless blob. So the three moon scenes all ask for "one cute round
     pale yellow full moon character", the style no longer mentions the sky, and the
     scenes in between are set under trees or indoors, where no sky shows.

     After the first complete render the verdict was that it did not show enough of
     the story: no girl, no frog, nobody laughing at the firefly, and lamps lit in a
     room that is meant to be dark. So the frog and the laughing animals now have
     their own pictures, the girl is shown after all, and the three bedroom scenes
     carry a `negative:` line, which replaces the project's negative prompt for that
     one picture: it lets people into the picture and keeps lamps out of it.

     The scene count is unchanged on purpose. A scene's picture depends on its
     position, so adding a scene would have redrawn every picture after it,
     including the good ones.

     "Shh" is written "Hush" because the voice model spells "Shh" out letter by letter. -->
