# The owl who could not hoot

<!-- Format: original story for young children (about 4 to 8), with one small animal
     hero, a problem, and a kind ending. Written for this project.

     Built on YouTube's own list of what makes good children's content: a complete
     story with a clear lesson, kindness, and feelings a child can recognise.

     Written to be joined in with, because that is what keeps a small child watching:
     it opens by asking the child a question, uses sounds to copy (Hoo! Twee! Hop!),
     repeats them, and ends by asking the child to whistle. The first picture is a big
     friendly face looking straight out, since the first second decides the swipe.

     Pip's look is set once, under `characters:` in config.yaml. Every scene whose
     narration says "Pip" gets that description added, which is what keeps Pip the
     same owl from picture to picture. Scenes that show Pip without naming her repeat
     the description in the picture line.

     Picture notes from the real runs. A pose the model cannot draw ("covering its
     face with both wings") came back as three different owls, so scenes with Pip ask
     for one owl in a simple pose. Colour and light words leak onto the owl: "night
     sky" gave it blue wings and "golden sparkles floating around it" turned it into a
     gold statue, so Pip's scenes reuse the opening's plain setting ("on a branch,
     full moon behind"). The first rabbit picture had two faces, one above the other;
     always look at every picture before showing this to a child.

     Scenes are kept to ten words or more where possible: the voice model's own notes
     say very short lines sound worse. -->

Can you hoot like an owl? Hoo! Hoo! Little Pip could not. Not even one tiny hoo.
<!-- image: close-up of the little owl looking at the viewer, smiling, on a branch, full moon behind -->
<!-- camera: zoom_in -->

Pip could only whistle. Twee! Twee! The other birds laughed, and Pip hid her face.
<!-- image: the little owl alone on a branch with its eyes closed, sad, full moon behind -->

Then one night, a thick, thick fog crept into the forest.
<!-- image: soft white fog filling a forest of tall trees at night, gentle moonlight from above -->
<!-- camera: pan_right -->

A baby rabbit was lost, and could not find her way home.
<!-- image: a single fluffy white baby rabbit sitting on the forest floor, looking up, misty forest behind -->

The big owls hooted. Hoo! Hoo! But the fog swallowed every sound.
<!-- image: two big brown grown-up owls side by side on a branch, beaks open, misty forest behind -->

So Pip took a big, big breath, and whistled! Twee! Twee! The sound flew right through the fog.
<!-- image: the little owl on a branch with its beak open, a bright ribbon of golden sparkles flying from it through the fog -->
<!-- camera: pan_right -->

Hop, hop, hop! The rabbit followed the whistle, all the way home.
<!-- image: a small fluffy baby rabbit hopping along a trail of golden sparkles through the fog toward a cozy burrow glowing with warm light -->

Now, when the fog comes, who do they call? Pip!
<!-- image: the happy little owl on a branch, a baby rabbit and little birds gathered around it, bright warm sunrise -->

Because the thing that makes you different can be the thing that helps.
<!-- image: a tiny round fluffy brown baby owl chick with huge shiny dark eyes flying happily, sunrise sky above the treetops -->
<!-- camera: zoom_out -->

Can you whistle like Pip? Try it!
<!-- image: close-up of the happy little owl looking at the viewer, smiling, on a branch, pink sunrise sky behind -->
<!-- camera: zoom_in -->
