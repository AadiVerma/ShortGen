# The keepers who vanished

<!-- Format: true unsolved mystery. Every claim below is in the National Records of
     Scotland account of the Flannan Isles lighthouse, December 1900. The popular
     details that are NOT in the records (a half-eaten meal, an overturned chair) are
     deliberately left out. -->

December, 1900. A ship reaches a lighthouse on a tiny Scottish island. Nobody comes out to meet it.
<!-- image: a tall white lighthouse on a small rocky island in a grey stormy sea, a small steamship approaching -->

Three keepers lived here. All three are gone.
<!-- image: the open door of a stone lighthouse seen from outside, dark and empty inside, wet stone steps -->
<!-- camera: zoom_in -->

Inside, the lamps are cleaned and ready to light. The kitchen is tidy.
<!-- image: the great glass lens of a lighthouse lamp, polished and unlit, grey daylight through the windows -->

Two sets of outdoor clothing are missing. Only two.
<!-- image: a row of iron coat hooks on a stone wall, a single heavy coat hanging, the other hooks empty -->

The last weather notes were written eleven days earlier.
<!-- image: an old open notebook and a slate on a wooden desk beside a cold oil lamp, dust in the air -->

Down at the landing, iron railings are twisted, and a stone block weighing over a ton has been torn loose.
<!-- image: twisted iron railings on a cliff edge above a violent sea, a huge square stone block lying broken on the rocks -->
<!-- camera: pan_down -->

The official answer: a giant wave took them.
<!-- image: an enormous dark wave rising high above a cliff with a small lighthouse on top -->

But no one saw it happen. And the three men were never found.
<!-- image: an empty grey sea at dusk, a distant lighthouse beam sweeping across the water -->
<!-- camera: zoom_out -->
