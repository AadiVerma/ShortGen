# One light-day

November 18th, 2026. Something humans built crosses a line no machine has ever crossed.
<!-- image: the Voyager space probe with a large white dish antenna and long thin metal arms, floating alone in black space, one tiny distant sun -->

Voyager 1 is now one light-day from Earth.
<!-- image: the Voyager space probe floating far away, very small in the frame, surrounded by an immense black starfield, one faint blue dot of light -->
<!-- camera: zoom_out -->

That means a radio signal, travelling at the speed of light, needs a full twenty-four hours to reach it.
<!-- image: a single thin beam of white light crossing black starry space from a small blue planet Earth into the far distance -->

It left in 1977, carrying a golden record of music and greetings from Earth.
<!-- image: a golden phonograph record disc with fine circular grooves floating in black space, starlight reflected on it -->

It has been flying for forty-nine years, at seventeen kilometers every second.
<!-- image: the planet Saturn and its wide rings, a small robotic space probe with a round dish antenna flying past in front of it -->
<!-- camera: pan_right -->

Its power fades a little every year. One day soon, it will go silent.
<!-- image: the Voyager space probe floating in black space as a dark silhouette, one faint orange light on it, only a few dim stars -->

But it will keep going, long after we are gone.
<!-- image: the Voyager space probe floating in black space, a small dark silhouette against the bright glowing band of the Milky Way -->

The farthest thing we ever made is still out there, saying hello.
