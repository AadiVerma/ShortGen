# The animal that ages backwards

<!-- Format: one surprising animal fact. Facts from the American Museum of Natural
     History's page on Turritopsis dohrnii: about 4.5 mm across, reverts to a polyp
     after injury or starvation, by transdifferentiation. The nickname overstates it:
     the animal can still be eaten or die of disease, and the script says so. -->

There is an animal that can age backwards.
<!-- image: one tiny translucent jellyfish glowing soft pink and blue in dark water -->

It is a jellyfish smaller than your fingernail.
<!-- image: a tiny glowing jellyfish floating beside a single small seashell that is much bigger than it -->

When it is hurt, or starving, it does not die.
<!-- image: a small torn jellyfish drifting downward through dark water, its glow fading -->
<!-- camera: pan_down -->

It sinks to the sea floor and turns back into its baby form.
<!-- image: a small glowing blob resting on a rock on the dark sea floor, tiny tendrils starting to grow from it -->

Its cells change jobs. A muscle cell can become a nerve cell.
<!-- image: glowing round cells changing color from red to electric blue, connected by fine threads of light -->

Then it grows up all over again.
<!-- image: a tiny new jellyfish lifting off from a thin stalk on a rock, rising through beams of light -->
<!-- camera: pan_up -->

In theory, it can repeat this forever.
<!-- image: a ring of small glowing jellyfish forming a perfect circle in dark water -->

It can still be eaten. But it may never die of old age.
<!-- image: a tiny glowing jellyfish in the foreground, the large dark shadow of a fish behind it -->

Scientists call it the immortal jellyfish.
<!-- image: one glowing jellyfish rising toward bright light at the surface of the sea -->
