# The day the Mona Lisa vanished

<!-- Each paragraph is one scene. The `image:` line under it describes the picture for that

In 1911, the Mona Lisa was just another painting in the Louvre.
<!-- image: a small Renaissance portrait of a smiling woman hanging among many paintings on a crowded museum wall, visitors in 1911 clothes walking past -->

Then, one Monday morning, a handyman named Vincenzo Peruggia lifted it off the wall.
<!-- image: a man in a white workman's smock lifting a small framed portrait off a museum wall in an empty gallery, early morning light -->

He had worked at the museum, so nobody looked twice at his white smock.
<!-- image: museum workers in white smocks crossing a grand gallery hall, 1911, one of them carrying a toolbox -->

He slipped the painting under his clothes and simply walked out.
<!-- image: a man in a white smock leaving a museum side door with a flat bundle hidden under his clothes, quiet Paris street -->

For a whole day, no one even noticed it was gone.
<!-- image: an empty space on a museum wall with four bare iron hooks, a guard dozing on a chair nearby -->

When the news broke, crowds lined up to stare at the empty wall.
<!-- image: a huge crowd in 1911 hats and coats queuing to stare at an empty spot on a museum wall -->

Police questioned everyone. Even Pablo Picasso was brought in.
<!-- image: a 1911 Paris police office, a desk lamp, stacks of case files, a detective pointing at a newspaper photo of an empty frame -->

For more than two years, the world's most wanted painting sat in a trunk in Paris.
<!-- image: a battered wooden trunk in a small shabby Paris room, a wrapped painting hidden inside under folded clothes -->

Peruggia was finally caught trying to sell it in Florence.
<!-- image: an art dealer's office in Florence, a man unwrapping a small painting on a table while two officials stare in shock -->

The painting went home. And the theft had made it the most famous face on Earth.
<!-- image: a small Renaissance portrait of a smiling woman behind a rope barrier on a museum wall, a sea of admirers in front of it -->
