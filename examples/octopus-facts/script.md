# Five facts about octopuses

An octopus has three hearts.
<!-- image: three bright red glowing hearts on the body of one cute orange octopus facing the viewer -->

Two pump blood through the gills. The third feeds the rest of the body.
<!-- image: close-up of an orange octopus, glowing red hearts in its body joined by flowing red ribbons of light -->

Its blood is blue, because it carries oxygen with copper instead of iron.
<!-- image: glowing electric blue veins branching across the head and arms of an orange octopus -->

Most of its neurons are not in its head. They are in its arms.
<!-- image: bright yellow lines of light running along every arm of an orange octopus like electric wires, sparks at the tips -->

Each arm can taste whatever it touches.
<!-- image: extreme close-up of a single orange tentacle with round white suckers wrapped around a white seashell, tiny sparkles -->

And with no bones at all, it can squeeze through a gap the size of a coin.
<!-- image: an orange octopus squeezing out of the narrow neck of a small glass bottle, half its body still inside -->

Eight arms, three hearts, blue blood. The ocean's strangest genius.
